Retirement Planning Overview: Bristol Myers Squibb

An educational guide for employees ages 50–65 preparing for the next chapter

What You Have Available

401(k) Plan

A workplace savings account where you set aside part of your pay, often with an employer contribution. Savings can grow over time.

Pension

An employer-funded benefit that may provide regular income in retirement based on your service and pay history.

ESPP

An Employee Stock Purchase Plan lets you buy company shares, sometimes at a discount, through payroll deductions.

How the Plan Works

Contributions: You choose a percentage of your paycheck to save in your 401(k). Workers age 50 and older may make extra "catch-up" contributions above the standard yearly IRS limit.

Employer Match: Many 401(k) plans add money when you contribute, up to a set amount.

Illustrative example only — not specific to your plan: An employer might match 100% of the first 3% you contribute, plus 50% of the next 2%. Check your official plan documents for your actual match.

Vesting: Vesting is how much of the employer money you keep if you leave. Your own contributions are always 100% yours.

Illustrative 3-year graded schedule — example only: 0% vested before 1 year, 33% after year 1, 66% after year 2, and 100% after year 3. Your actual schedule may differ.

Key Retirement Considerations

Diversification

Spreading savings across different types of investments may help manage risk. Holding too much in a single stock — including company stock through an ESPP — can increase risk.

Tax Treatment

Pre-tax 401(k) contributions lower taxable income now but are taxed when withdrawn. Roth contributions are taxed now but qualified withdrawals may be tax-free later.

Questions to Ask Before You Retire

  • How much monthly income will I need to cover my expenses?
  • When should I claim Social Security, and how does waiting affect my benefit?
  • Am I fully vested in my employer contributions?
  • How will my pension be paid — as a lump sum or monthly income?
  • Is my savings spread across enough types of investments, or concentrated in company stock?
  • What are the tax effects of my pre-tax versus Roth savings?
  • Do I have a plan for health coverage between retirement and Medicare at 65?
