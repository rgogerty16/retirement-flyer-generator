Retirement Planning Overview: Johnson & Johnson

An educational guide for employees ages 50–65 preparing for retirement

What You Have Available

401(k) Plan

  • A workplace savings account you fund through paycheck contributions.
  • You choose how much to save and how it is invested.
  • Savings can grow tax-advantaged over time.

Pension Plan

  • A benefit that may provide regular income in retirement.
  • Typically based on factors like pay and years of service.
  • Review your plan documents for your specific benefit details.

How the Plan Works

Most 401(k) plans share three common features: contributions, an employer match, and a vesting schedule. The examples below show how these often work in practice.

  • Contributions: You set a percentage of your pay to save. The IRS sets annual contribution limits, and savers age 50+ can make additional "catch-up" contributions.
  • Employer match: Many employers add money to your account based on what you save.
  • Vesting: "Vesting" means how much of the employer contributions you fully own over time.
Illustrative example — not actual plan terms: An employer might match 50% of your contributions up to 6% of pay. A typical 3-year graded vesting schedule could vest employer contributions at 33% after year 1, 66% after year 2, and 100% after year 3. Your own contributions are always 100% yours. Confirm your real match and vesting details in your official plan documents.

Key Retirement Considerations

  • Diversification: Spreading savings across different types of investments may help manage risk. A mix that fits your timeline and comfort with risk is a common starting point.
  • Tax treatment: Pre-tax (Traditional) contributions lower taxable income now, but withdrawals are taxed later. Roth contributions are taxed now, but qualified withdrawals can be tax-free. Many plans offer both.
  • Income planning: Think about how 401(k) savings, pension income, and Social Security may work together to cover your future expenses.

Questions to Ask Before You Retire

  • How much monthly income will I need to cover living and health care costs?
  • Am I fully vested in my employer contributions, and what is my pension benefit?
  • When does it make the most sense for me to claim Social Security?
  • Is my mix of investments still right for my age and goals?
  • Should I use pre-tax savings, Roth savings, or both?
  • How will required minimum distributions (RMDs) affect my taxes later?
  • Do I have an emergency fund and a plan for unexpected expenses?
