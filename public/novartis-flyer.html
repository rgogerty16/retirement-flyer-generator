Retirement Planning Overview:
Novartis

For employees ages 50–65 nearing retirement. This guide summarizes key retirement benefits and planning considerations — so you can make informed decisions about your financial future.
What You Have Available
💼
401(k) Savings Plan
Tax-advantaged contributions with employer matching. Pre-tax and Roth (after-tax) options available.
🏛️
Pension / Cash Balance Plan
Company-funded retirement account. Your balance grows with annual pay credits and interest credits.

Purchase Novartis shares at a discount. Shares may be held or sold — consider diversification timing.

Plan availability may vary. Refer to your Novartis Benefits Portal or HR for current plan details specific to your role and location.

How the 401(k) Plan Works
  • Your Contributions: You elect a percentage of your salary — up to IRS limits ($23,500 in 2025; $31,000 if age 50+).
  • Employer Match (Illustrative): Novartis has historically provided a generous match — commonly structured as 100% on the first 5% of eligible pay. Verify your current match in the Benefits Portal.
  • Vesting Schedule: Company matching contributions typically vest on a graded schedule (e.g., 20% per year over 5 years). If you have 5+ years of service, you are likely fully vested.
  • Catch-Up Contributions: If you are age 50 or older, you can contribute an additional $7,500 per year (2025 limit) — a significant opportunity to accelerate savings.
  • Investment Options: Choose from a range of mutual funds, index funds, and target-date funds designed for specific retirement years.
Cash Balance Pension Highlights
  • Annual Pay Credits: Each year, Novartis credits a percentage of your eligible pay into your cash balance account.
  • Interest Credits: Your balance earns guaranteed interest credits annually, regardless of market performance.
  • Payout Options: At retirement, you may typically choose a lump sum or annuity (monthly income for life). Evaluate both options carefully.
  • Fully Vested: Most employees become fully vested in the pension after 3 years of service.
  • Pension + 401(k): Having both a defined-benefit pension and a defined-contribution 401(k) is a significant advantage — they provide complementary income streams in retirement.

Pension terms may vary by hire date and employment category. Review your Pension Statement for your personal projected benefit.

Key Retirement Planning Concepts
Diversification

Don't concentrate too heavily in any single investment — including Novartis stock. As you approach retirement, consider gradually shifting toward lower-volatility holdings (bonds, stable-value funds) to protect your accumulated savings.

Pre-Tax vs. Roth 401(k)

Pre-tax: Contributions reduce today's taxable income; withdrawals are taxed in retirement.
Roth: No upfront tax break; qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. Consider your expected tax rate in retirement when choosing.

Retirement Income Planning

Think in terms of income sources: Pension + 401(k) + Social Security + personal savings. Map out when each source turns on, how much it provides, and how long it needs to last. A common guideline: plan for 25–30 years of retirement income.

Questions to Ask Before You Retire
  • Am I maximizing my 401(k) contributions — including catch-up contributions if I'm 50 or older?
  • Is the employer match fully vested — and am I leaving any match on the table?
  • What is my estimated monthly pension benefit, and should I take a lump sum or annuity?
  • When should I claim Social Security — at 62, 67, or 70 — to maximize my lifetime benefit?
  • What is my target retirement date, and do I have at least 12–18 months to prepare?
  • Have I updated my 401(k) investment allocation as I get closer to retirement?
  • How much Novartis stock do I hold (ESPP or otherwise) — and is it appropriately diversified?
  • What are my healthcare coverage options between retirement and Medicare eligibility at 65?
