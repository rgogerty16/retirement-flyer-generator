Retirement Planning Overview: Daiichi Sankyo

An educational guide for employees ages 50–65 preparing for retirement

What You Have Available

Daiichi Sankyo offers a 401(k) retirement savings plan. This plan helps you set aside money from your paycheck to build savings for retirement.

  • You choose how much of your pay to contribute, up to IRS yearly limits.
  • If you are age 50 or older, you can make extra "catch-up" contributions above the standard limit.
  • Your savings grow over time and can be invested across different options inside the plan.

How the Plan Works

Your Contributions

You decide a percentage of your salary to save. Contributions are taken automatically from each paycheck. Review your contribution rate yearly, especially in your final working years.

Employer Match Illustrative

Many employers match part of what you save. A common example: 100% of the first 3% you contribute, plus 50% of the next 2%. Check your official plan documents for actual terms.

Vesting Illustrative

"Vesting" means how much of the employer match you keep if you leave. Your own contributions are always 100% yours. A typical 3-year graded schedule might look like: 33% after year 1, 66% after year 2, 100% after year 3. Confirm your real schedule in your plan summary.

Key Retirement Considerations

  • Diversification: Spreading money across different types of investments (such as stocks, bonds, and cash) can help manage risk. As retirement nears, many people shift toward a more balanced mix.
  • Tax treatment: Traditional (pre-tax) contributions lower your taxable income now, but withdrawals are taxed later. Roth contributions (if offered) are taxed now, but qualified withdrawals are tax-free in retirement.
  • Income planning: Think about how your savings, Social Security, and any other income will work together to cover monthly expenses in retirement.

Questions to Ask Before You Retire

  • How much monthly income will I need to cover my expenses in retirement?
  • Am I fully vested in my employer contributions?
  • Is my investment mix still appropriate for my age and goals?
  • When should I start claiming Social Security to fit my plan?
  • Will my savings be taxed as pre-tax, Roth, or a mix — and how does that affect withdrawals?
  • Have I planned for healthcare costs before and after Medicare eligibility?
  • Do I understand the rules for required minimum distributions (RMDs) later in retirement?
